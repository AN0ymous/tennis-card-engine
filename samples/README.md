# Reference listings

Real card photos supplied by the owner, used as the test set for the serial
rules and as reference examples for the photo-reading step. `cases.json`
records what each one shows and the verdict the engine must reach.

What the first batch established, in the owner's words: the legitimate serial
is a **metallic foil stamp**. It can be on the front or the back, and its size
varies by set. It is distinct from the card's checklist number, which is flat
printed ink and part of the design (the "162" on the Bublik back, the "R2" on
the Jovic back). A card can carry both, and only the foil stamp is the serial.

One exception to the sheen test, from case 7: **early NetPro cards (2003
Court Authentic and its kin) carry the serial as a dark stamp, printed
vertically up the left edge of the back, that can look flat rather than
metallic in a photo. It is still a legitimate serial.** What separates it
from a printed "1 of N" is that it is a stamped, per-card unique number in
the NetPro position, not a run statement repeated on every card.

The fourth case is the trap. A card can carry a flat-printed "1 of 2000"
(or "1 of 100"): that is the print-run statement, printed identically on every
card in the run, and it is not a serial -- the card is not #1 of anything. Only
a foil stamp with a unique number is a serial. Slab labels add more numbers
that are not serials either: PSA certificate numbers, barcodes, QR codes.

Illegitimate cards, from the owner's knowledge of the market:

- **Ace Authentic with an on-card autograph is fake.** Ace Authentic was not
  able to produce on-card autographs, so ink on the card surface -- as
  opposed to a signed sticker -- means a forgery, regardless of hand
  numbering, a grading slab, a PSA/DNA certificate, or an auction sale.
  A sticker autograph from them is how they were made and is allowed, as is
  any Ace Authentic card without an autograph whose numbering is stamped.
  The engine settles it from the listing text when it says "sticker" or
  "on-card", from the photos when the photo step is on, and otherwise lets
  the card through with a check-by-eye note.
- **Numbering on a round holographic circle sticker is fake.** Case 6 shows
  the type: a circular Ace Authentic hologram sticker in a corner of the
  front with "8/10" written on it. A genuine serial is stamped into the card;
  a number that sits on a sticker is a rejection on its own, whatever else
  the card carries.
- **Hand-written numbering is not a serial.** Only a foil stamp is.

The eighth case is the buyback exception. NetPro base, glossy and Elite
glossy cards are excluded, **unless the card is a buyback with gold metallic
numbering**: an unnumbered base card NetPro bought back and stamped in gold
foil (here a 1991 Tour Star Agassi stamped 1/1 for the 20th anniversary,
sealed in a case with a "20th NETPRO" seal). Those are genuine and wanted.
The engine lets them through when the listing says buyback or 20th
anniversary, and the photo step can confirm the gold foil stamp.

Buckets still wanted: checklist-number traps (a printed number that looks
like a serial but isn't), and illegitimate listings (customs, reprints,
unnumbered cards listed as 1/1, lot listings) with a note on how to tell.
